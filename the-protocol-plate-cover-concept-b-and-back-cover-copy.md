# The Protocol Plate — Cover Decision: Concept B

**Status:** Approved for production. Ship Concept B.

## Why B, not A or C

Our reader isn't browsing the carnivore shelf cold. She's diet-fatigued — she already bought and abandoned an AIP book and a keto book that looked almost exactly like Concept A. Matching the loudest genre convention risks reading as "another one of those" in the half-second she has to judge a thumbnail.

Concept C solves the *message* problem (it signals "this one's different") but breaks category recognition badly enough that at a 100px white-on-white thumbnail it may not register as a diet book at all without a second look. That's a real cost at the size most of these covers are actually seen.

Concept B is the compromise that keeps both jobs alive:

- **Category recognition, kept.** Dark ground, red-adjacent accent, a cut of meat — everything a carnivore-category shopper's eye is already trained to find in a thumbnail grid.
- **Differentiation, via typography instead of imagery.** The name-plate title treatment and serif do the work Concept C tries to do with imagery alone. It reads as a program handed down by an authority — a protocol — not a shouting cookbook. That's the exact promise this reader hasn't been able to trust yet, and it's a promise a font and a layout choice can make without sacrificing the thumbnail read.

## Cover Spec

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Background | Near-black / dark charcoal ground — matches category convention |
| Accent color | Red-adjacent (deep crimson / blood-orange), used sparingly — rule lines, a single word, the plate border |
| Hero image | Single, well-lit cut of meat, shot plainly (not styled as a "recipe" shot) — restraint signals clinical/authority, not indulgence |
| Title treatment | Engraved name-plate motif — title set in serif, framed like a brass plaque or clinical placard, not a hand-lettered or bold sans "cookbook" logotype |
| Subtitle | Small caps or light serif beneath the plate, secondary in weight to the plate itself |
| Overall read at 100px thumbnail | Dark ground + meat + red accent must still register as "carnivore diet book" at a glance; the plate is the differentiator once she looks twice |

## Test: Pull Concept C's language onto Concept B's cover

Concept C's subtitle line — *"every food you reintroduce, ranked: trigger, unclear, cleared"* — is the sharpest, most concrete promise across all three concepts. It doesn't need Concept C's imagery to work; it needs a place to land. Recommended placement is the back cover and an A+ content module, not the front cover plate itself, so Concept B's front-of-book thumbnail stays clean and fast-reading.

### Back cover copy (draft)

> **You don't need another elimination diet. You need to know what's actually safe to eat again.**
>
> Most carnivore and AIP books stop at the hard part — cut everything out, wait, hope you feel better. They leave you standing at the reintroduction phase with no system, guessing whether last Thursday's flare came from the ghee, the black pepper, or a bad night's sleep.
>
> *The Protocol Plate* picks up where those books quit. Inside, every food you reintroduce gets ranked: **trigger, unclear, or cleared** — a clear verdict, tracked on paper, so you're never relying on memory to make a call your body already made three days ago.
>
> This isn't a cookbook. It's a protocol — the one your last two attempts didn't give you.

### A+ content module (draft)

**Module: "Know Exactly Where Every Food Stands"**

- Headline: *Every food you reintroduce, ranked: trigger, unclear, cleared.*
- Subhead: No more guessing which food caused the flare. A structured tracker turns "I think it was the eggs" into a documented, three-way verdict you can trust.
- Supporting visual: a simplified version of the book's reintroduction tracker table, three columns — **Trigger / Unclear / Cleared** — with 2–3 example foods in each column.
- CTA line: *Stop restarting elimination from scratch. Start knowing.*
